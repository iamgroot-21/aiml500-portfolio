Title

Evolution of AI and Machine Learning: 1950 to Today

Objective

This timeline traces the major milestones in artificial intelligence and machine learning from Alan Turing's foundational 1950 paper through the 2024 Nobel Prizes awarded for AI-related research. By mapping these events chronologically, the artifact helps the audience understand how cycles of enthusiasm and disillusionment (the two AI winters) shaped the field, and how the convergence of algorithms, data, and compute after 2012 triggered the current era of generative AI.

Eras of AI

Birth of AI 1950–1973 Winter 1 Expert Systems 1980–1986 Winter 2 ML, Data & Cloud 1993–2011 Deep Learning & Cloud 2012–2021 Generative AI 2022–

Timeline

1950
Turing Test
Alan Turing proposes a test of machine intelligence, the first benchmark for "thinking" machines.
1956
Dartmouth Workshop
John McCarthy coins "artificial intelligence." AI becomes its own research field.
1958
Perceptron
Frank Rosenblatt demonstrates the perceptron, an early neural network that learns from examples.
1966
ELIZA
Weizenbaum's MIT chatbot shows how readily people respond to conversational machines.
1973
First AI Winter Begins
Perceptrons (1969) and the UK Lighthill Report criticize AI's progress. Governments cut funding.
1980
Expert Systems Boom
Rule-based systems like XCON reach industry at DEC and draw heavy corporate investment.
1986
Backpropagation
Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams popularize a practical way to train multilayer neural networks.
1987
Second AI Winter Begins
The Lisp machine market collapses. Expert systems prove costly and brittle, and funding dries up.
1997
Deep Blue
IBM's Deep Blue beats chess champion Garry Kasparov, proving machines can win at complex problems.
2006
Cloud Computing Arrives
Amazon launches S3 and EC2, making large-scale computing rentable. Hinton revives deep learning.
2007
NVIDIA CUDA
NVIDIA lets GPUs run general computing, which later speeds up neural network training.
2009
ImageNet
Fei-Fei Li's team releases millions of labeled images, giving deep learning the data it needs.
2011
Watson Wins Jeopardy!
IBM Watson beats human champions, showing AI can handle open-ended language questions.
2012
AlexNet
Krizhevsky, Sutskever, and Hinton win ImageNet with a deep CNN on NVIDIA GPUs. The deep learning boom begins.
2014
DeepMind and GANs
Google acquires DeepMind. Ian Goodfellow introduces GANs, which generate realistic images.
2015
OpenAI Founded
OpenAI starts as a research lab aiming to keep advanced AI open and beneficial.
2016
AlphaGo
DeepMind's AlphaGo beats Go champion Lee Sedol, a game long thought too intuitive for machines.
2017
Transformers
Google's "Attention Is All You Need" introduces the architecture behind modern language models.
2020
GPT-3
OpenAI's 175-billion-parameter GPT-3 writes fluent text from short prompts.
2022
ChatGPT
ChatGPT launches Nov. 30 and reaches about 100 million users in roughly two months.
2024
Nobel Prizes
Hinton and Hopfield (Physics) and Hassabis, Jumper, and Baker (Chemistry) win Nobels for AI-related work.

Analysis: The AI Winters

First winter (about 1974–1980). Early systems such as perceptrons hit hard limits, computers were too weak, and promises outran results. The UK and U.S. governments cut research funding and many labs shrank.

Second winter (about 1987–1993). Expert systems were expensive to maintain and broke outside narrow cases, and the specialized Lisp machine market collapsed. Companies failed, funding fell again, and researchers avoided the "AI" label while neural networks and statistical methods kept advancing quietly.

Analysis: Why Deep Learning Took Off After 2012

Three things came together: algorithms (backpropagation, CNNs, transformers), data (the web and ImageNet), and compute (NVIDIA GPUs and cloud platforms such as AWS). Our World in Data shows training computation doubling about every six months since 2010. Google's PaLM (2022) used more than 5 million times the training compute of AlexNet (2012). Hardware mattered as much as theory: the transistor, the microprocessor, and the GPU made today's models possible.

Process

I researched AI milestones through three primary sources: a YouTube documentary by Putchuon covering the last 100 years of AI history, an IEEE Computer Society article on AI's evolution, and Max Roser's "Brief History of Artificial Intelligence" on Our World in Data. I synthesized these into a single chronological narrative, then designed the timeline layout in Microsoft PowerPoint to present the milestones visually with era groupings and analysis sections.
